# A Review on Gravitons

A theoretical review of the graviton as the hypothetical quantum mediator of gravity, covering its theoretical foundations, expected properties, experimental challenges, and recent approaches to probing quantum aspects of gravity.

The project begins by motivating the quantization of gravity through the unification of fundamental interactions, the singularities of general relativity, and the conceptual tension between time in quantum theory and general relativity. It then reviews the historical development of quantum gravity and outlines the covariant, canonical, and sum-over-histories approaches.

The expected properties of the graviton are examined through the analogy between electromagnetic and gravitational interactions. In the weak-field description, the gravitational field is represented by a rank-two tensor coupled to the energy-momentum tensor, motivating the identification of the graviton as a massless spin-2 quantum field.

The review then discusses major obstacles to quantum gravity, particularly the Planck scale, perturbative non-renormalizability, and the extreme difficulty of directly detecting individual gravitons. The latter is illustrated through estimates of gravitational interaction strengths and detector requirements.

Finally, the project surveys several directions in contemporary research, including gravitational waves, string theory, and gravitationally induced entanglement. In particular, it discusses how entanglement generated solely through gravitational interactions could provide a probe of the quantum nature of gravity.
